## 2. Company Overview

### Business and reportable segments

The FY2025 10-K describes The Boeing Company as one of the world's major aerospace firms. It is organized around the products and services it offers[^15]. The company reports three segments: Commercial Airplanes (BCA), Defense, Space & Security (BDS) and Global Services (BGS)[^15].

- **Commercial Airplanes** develops, produces and markets commercial jet aircraft, mainly to airlines worldwide. Its production family is the 737 narrow-body and the 767, 777 and 787 wide-bodies. Development continues on the 777X and on the 737-7 and 737-10 derivatives[^15].
- **Defense, Space & Security** covers manned and unmanned military aircraft and weapons systems, vertical lift and commercial derivative aircraft such as anti-submarine and tanker aircraft. It also covers strategic defense and intelligence systems, satellite systems and space exploration[^15].
- **Global Services** serves commercial and defense customers worldwide. Its offerings include supply chain and logistics management, engineering, maintenance and modifications, upgrades and conversions, and spare parts[^15].

The 10-K reports 182,000 employees for FY2025[^15]. Headquarters are in Arlington, Virginia[^15].

## 3. Products, Services and Technology

### Portfolio by segment (10-K)

- **Commercial Airplanes (BCA).** Boeing reports three segments: Commercial Airplanes, Defense, Space & Security (BDS) and Global Services (BGS)[^15].
  - BCA's in-production family comprises the 737 narrow-body and the 767, 777 and 787 wide-bodies[^15].
  - Development continues on the 777X and on the 737-7 and 737-10 derivatives[^15].
- **Defense, Space & Security (BDS).** BDS covers several groups of products[^15]:
  - manned and unmanned military aircraft, including fighters and trainers;
  - vertical lift;
  - commercial-derivative aircraft such as anti-submarine and tanker platforms;
  - strategic missile and defense systems, C4ISR and cyber;
  - government and commercial satellites and space exploration.
  - The 10-K names the F/A-18, F-15, T-7A and MQ-25 among its programs[^15].
- **Global Services (BGS).** BGS provides supply-chain and logistics management, engineering, maintenance and modifications, conversions, spare parts, training systems, technical documentation and digital analytics[^15].
- **Company-wide metrics.** Total backlog reached a record $715 billion at the end of Q2 2026[^3]. Management said on the Q2 call that production and deliveries were at levels not seen since 2018[^4].
- **Company claims.** Boeing's website describes its freighters as serving more than 90 percent of the global air freight market[^16]. That is a company claim, not an independently verified figure.

### Certification and regulatory milestones

- **737 MAX and 787 airworthiness certificates.** In July 2026 the FAA authorized Boeing to resume issuing airworthiness certificates for all 737 MAX and 787 airplanes, per the Q2 call[^4]. Management described its commercial certification programs as "on plan"[^3][^4].
- **737-7 and 737-10.** On May 28, 2026, FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said he expected the 737 MAX to be certified by year-end 2026. He expected the MAX 7 first, then the -10[^17].
- **777-9.** Bedford said he hoped to certify the 777X early in 2027[^17].
  - FlightGlobal reported on June 7, 2026 that Boeing was cleared for the next phase of 777-9 certification[^18].
  - Aviation Week reported on July 16, 2026 that the 777-9 had completed more than half its certification flight tests, with remaining work on ETOPS, pilot training and production aircraft[^19].
  - In September 2025, Aviation Week described the 777-9 as roughly six years late[^20].

> Conflict: In September 2025 Aviation Week reported that Boeing had promised Lufthansa its first 777-9 in June 2026[^20]. In May 2026 the FAA Administrator pointed to certification in early 2027[^17]. The sources reviewed do not confirm a revised first-delivery date.

### Next-generation awards and competitive position

- **USAF F-47 (NGAD).** Boeing won the Air Force's sixth-generation fighter contract in March 2025, beating Lockheed Martin[^6][^21].
  - The aircraft, designated F-47, has entered engineering and manufacturing development, with first flight expected as early as 2028[^6].

- **Navy F/A-XX.** On September 29, 2026, the Navy awarded Boeing the F/A-XX full-scale development contract[^6][^7].

  - Northrop Grumman was the only other remaining bidder[^21].
  - The F/A-XX is to augment and then replace F/A-18E/F Super Hornets and EA-18G Growlers beginning in the 2030s, operating alongside Lockheed Martin's F-35C[^7].
- **Commercial competition.** The 10-K names Airbus as the principal commercial competitor[^15].
  - Airbus delivered 136 aircraft in December 2025, comprising 114 narrowbodies and 22 widebodies[^22].
  - The sources reviewed give no matching Boeing monthly figure or model-by-model comparison, such as 737 MAX versus A320neo.
- **Defense competition.** The 10-K's defense competitor list includes General Dynamics, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, RTX and BAE Systems[^15].

### Facts behind the SWOT

**Strengths.** Boeing describes itself as a leading producer of commercial aircraft with a jetliner family serving passenger and cargo requirements.[^15] In 2025, 35% of revenues came from U.S. government contracts, including FMS, which gives the company a defense revenue base alongside its commercial business.[^15]

**Weaknesses.** The 10-K states that Boeing depends heavily on commercial airlines.[^15] The Q2 2026 release lists production quality issues, aircraft production health and the ability to certify new aircraft among its risk factors.[^3] StockIntent cites a Q2 2026 operating margin of 0.6% on $24.6 billion of revenue.[^41]

**Opportunities.** The 10-K states that airline financial performance is benefiting from resilient travel demand.[^15] Record total and commercial backlogs were reported at Q2 2026.[^3]

**Threats.** The 10-K flags competition as a risk to future contracts and sales.[^15] It also flags changes in contract accounting estimates as a risk to future results.[^15] The Q2 2026 release lists further risks:
- dependence on subcontractors and suppliers[^3]
- reliance on fixed-price contracts[^3]
- dependence on U.S. government contracts[^3]
- tariffs and trade restrictions[^3]
- realizing benefits from the Spirit AeroSystems acquisition[^3]

### Perspectives

- **Zacks** (via Yahoo Finance) describes Boeing as one of the largest U.S. aircraft manufacturers by revenues, orders and deliveries.[^44] It notes that Boeing's defense unit booked $15 billion in Q4 2025.[^44]
- **Alpha Spread** rates Boeing's moat as wide, citing pilot-training and maintenance switching costs and certification barriers to newcomers.[^45] It notes that Airbus could erode this position by funding customers' fleet-transition costs.[^45]
- **StockIntent** rates Boeing's moat as narrow with a stable-to-weakening trend.[^41] It attributes the weakening to factory bottlenecks and Airbus order momentum offsetting defense wins.[^41]

## 6. Strategy and Growth

*Caveat: targets are management's own statements as of the date shown for each row; consensus is a single aggregator page (not a licensed feed) cited with its date, and the gap is derived from the range midpoint.*

### Stated strategy and safety-and-quality framing
On the Q2 2026 call (2026-07-28), CEO Kelly Ortberg presented the safety and quality plan as the governing constraint on production, saying Boeing "will only move production forward when the system and our supply chain are ready."[^4] The same plan that management credits for results in Renton will guide the new Everett 737 line.[^4] Management cited a record backlog of more than 6,200 airplanes as the demand base for the ramp.[^4] It also cited a 20-year market outlook of nearly 44,000 new aircraft.[^4][^46] Vertical integration of fuselage supply followed the completion of the Spirit AeroSystems acquisition on 2025-12-08.[^23] On the Q2 call, management said the Wichita integration was meeting targeted reductions in quality defects before fuselages ship to final assembly.[^4]

### Production-rate and certification targets (all set 2026-07-28, Q2 call)
- **737:** Boeing is ramping to 47 per month after a capstone review in May 2026, and expects factory rollouts to reach 47 per month in summer 2026.[^4] Low-rate MAX production began on the Everett North Line in July 2026, which management said enables the next planned rate break of 52 per month.[^4] For context, Forecast International counted 32 737 MAX aircraft produced in November 2025.[^47]
- **787:** Production has stabilized at 8 per month after a temporary slowdown in April 2026 to let parts of the supply chain recover.[^4] Management tied the timing of rate 10 to GE engine-delivery recovery over the summer.[^4] No date was given for rate 10.[^4]
- **737-7/737-10 certification:** On the call, management said 737-7 testing was complete and an amended FAA type certificate was expected "very soon."[^4] It said 737-10 certification would follow the 737-7.[^4] Boeing subsequently published "FAA certifies Boeing 737-10" in October 2026.[^16]
- **Flight-test program:** Management said more than 55% of certification flight testing under phased TIAs was complete, and that it expected approval to start ETOPS testing later in 2026.[^4] The call excerpt reviewed does not name the program.[^4]

### Defense de-risking and financial trajectory
In Q2 2026, BDS achieved Milestone C on both the T-7 and the MQ-25 programs, which authorizes low-rate initial production.[^4] Management described the T-7 as moving to a production-ready configuration that reduces risk.[^4] For Q2 2026, Boeing reported an operating margin of 0.6% and positive free cash flow of $631M.[^4] Those figures are reported results, not forward targets. On 2026-08-24, Boeing entered a $3.0 billion, 364-day revolving credit agreement with Citibank, N.A.[^48]

For FY2026, the Yahoo analysis page (2026-10-21) shows consensus revenue of $97.5 billion and consensus EPS of –$1.10. Management has set no revenue or EPS target for comparison in the sources reviewed.[^4]

### Capex and capacity
Trailing-twelve-month capital expenditures reached $3.849 billion as of June 2026, the five-year peak.[^49] That compares with an FY2021–2025 average of $1.78 billion and $2.23 billion in FY2024.[^49] Derived: TTM capex is about 2.2x the five-year average ($3.849B ÷ $1.78B). *Caveat: Deal rows are extracted by pattern from 8-K, 10-K/10-Q note and press text; values are as stated at announcement (cash consideration where the source says so), not adjusted for closing terms, and rationale cells quote the source wording rather than assess the deal.*

### Deal pattern
Boeing's recent portfolio activity centres on one large acquisition, Spirit AeroSystems, and one disclosed divestiture agreement[^51][^52]. Most other recent Item 1.01 8-Ks cover financing or legal matters rather than M&A. These include a $3.0 billion 364-day credit agreement in each of August 2025 and August 2026, an October 2024 underwritten common-stock offering and a May 2025 Non-Prosecution Agreement[^48][^53][^54][^55]. Over a longer horizon, Tracxn counts 27 Boeing acquisitions across 20 sectors, including aviation IT, IT services and drones[^56].

### Spirit AeroSystems: terms, close and segment impact
Boeing announced a definitive all-stock agreement on July 1, 2024. The deal valued Spirit's equity at about $4.7 billion ($37.25 per share) and carried a total transaction value of about $8.3 billion including Spirit's last reported net debt. The exchange ratio ranged from 0.18 to 0.25 Boeing shares per Spirit share, set by a $149.00–$206.94 VWAP collar[^51]. Implied net debt assumed is about $3.6 billion (derived: $8.3 billion total value minus $4.7 billion equity value).

The acquisition closed on December 8, 2025[^57]. Spirit's major operations moved into Commercial Airplanes and its aftermarket business into Global Services. Its defense programs now operate independently as Spirit Defense[^57]. The 10-K states that the Spirit Acquisition significantly affected Boeing's financial position, results of operations and cash flows[^15]. Both 2026 10-Qs carry a dedicated Spirit Acquisition note (Note 2)[^58][^59].

### Management rationale
Management presented the Spirit deal as a safety and quality measure. It said reintegration would align commercial production systems, Safety and Quality Management Systems and the workforce around shared priorities[^51]. The stated aims also included continuity for U.S. defense programs and supply-chain stability[^51]. At closing, management stressed stability and continued delivery while integration proceeds[^57].

### Divestiture: JNPR Aero / Project Maroon
On April 22, 2025, Boeing signed a Membership Interest Purchase Agreement with JNPR Aero, LLC and the buyer, Project Maroon, LLC[^52]. The portion of the 8-K reviewed does not name the assets sold, the price, the segment affected or the closing date. ## 12. Investment Thesis and Debates

*Caveat: No sell-side research or expert-call transcripts were available; firm names appear only where a cited transcript or page names the firm, rating actions are as listed on the aggregator page on the date shown, analyst questions are quoted as asked, not as views, and management answers are the company's statements on the same call, quoted from the same transcript.*

### What analysts asked and what management answered

**Wolfe Research (Q3 2025): 777X cash burn.** Myles Walton asked about the 777X's 2026 cash burn. Jay Malave said the program is a $2 billion headwind against prior expectations, and that absolute cash usage will be "a little bit higher than that." He described 2026 as a heavy-use year, with improvement in 2027, near-neutral cash in 2028 and positive program cash from 2029.[^77]

**Bank of America and Baird (Q3 2025): 777X delay.** Ortberg said there are "no new issues with the airplane itself or the engines." He attributed the delay solely to completing certification work, including obtaining Type Inspection Authorization (TIA) so that test flights earn certification credit.[^77] He added that hardware and software modifications to test aircraft are needed and that anti-ice remains the critical path.[^77]

**Goldman Sachs (Q3 2025): 737 ramp.** Ortberg cautioned that rate 42 is a factory flow rate, so monthly rollouts will not always equal 42.[^77]

**Bernstein (Q4 2025): hardest parts of the plan.** Ortberg said the 737 line reached an effective rate of 42 in November and December. He added that those were holiday-heavy months and that stability still had to be shown.[^78]

**Baird (Q4 2025): 2026 deliveries.** Mollave guided to about 500 737 deliveries in 2026, compared with 447 in 2025. He said only one aircraft remained carried over from inventory.[^78]

**Wolfe Research (Q4 2025): cash timing.** Walton asked how long excess advances and customer considerations would weigh on cash. Mollave declined to break them out. He said excess advances burn down faster than 737/787 considerations, and that both depend on reaching higher production rates.[^78]

**Citi (Q4 2025): free cash flow.** Mollave repeated that $10 billion of free cash flow is "very attainable," including the Spirit impact.[^78]

**Jefferies (Q4 2025): BCA margins.** Mollave said 737 and 787 cash margins are depressed and should improve given the backlog. He put the Spirit cash impact at negative $1 billion for the year.[^78]

**JPMorgan (Q4 2025): defense.** Ortberg called the tanker charge "discrete." He said it was driven mainly by higher 767 commercial production cost and does not reflect on other BDS programs.[^78]

**October target cuts.** Reasons for the October cuts would show whether they reflect a common new concern.[^83]

### DoD reporting lag and the 12-month window

The 12-month window ends 2026-10-21[^112]. DoD contract actions are published on USAspending only after a 90-day delay. As a result, DoD actions signed in roughly the last quarter of the window are likely missing or incomplete.

The lag matters because DoD dominates the window:

- DoD sub-agencies account for about $26,310m of the $27,710m 12-month total, about 95%[^112].
  - Derived: Air Force + Navy + Army + Defense Logistics Agency + SOCOM + Missile Defense Agency + Defense Information Systems Agency + Defense Contract Management Agency.
- Non-DoD agencies, almost entirely NASA, account for about $1,400m[^112].

The lag therefore affects nearly the whole 12-month figure, which should be read as a floor for DoD activity in that period[^112]. The 3-year and 5-year windows carry the same 90-day gap at their ends. The gap is a smaller fraction of those longer windows[^113][^114].
