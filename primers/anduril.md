## 3. Products, Services and Technology

### Lattice software and counter-UAS (status: fielded)
- The U.S. Army awarded Anduril an enterprise contract worth up to $20 billion over 10 years, announced in March 2026[^3][^15].
- The contract consolidates more than 120 separate procurement actions into one vehicle, with task orders for Anduril software, hardware and data infrastructure available through 2036[^15].
- The Pentagon's Joint Interagency Task Force–401 selected Lattice as its enterprise tactical command-and-control platform for counter-UAS, with an initial task order reportedly valued at about $87 million[^15].
- Lattice fuses data from radars, electro-optical sensors, electronic warfare systems and kinetic interceptors into a single operating picture[^15].
- Anduril announced its first counter-drone system in October 2019[^14].
- Its Sentry tower is deployed in California[^12].
- The sources reviewed name no competing C-UAS command-and-control product.

### Autonomous air systems
**YFQ-44A / FQ-44 Fury (status: flight test; low-rate production started)**
- The YFQ-44A is one of two aircraft selected for the Air Force's CCA Increment 1, alongside General Atomics' YFQ-42A[^16][^17].
- It first flew on October 31, 2025[^18][^19].
- At least three have been built[^19].
- In February 2026 the Air Force began captive-carry weapons integration with an inert AIM-120 AMRAAM[^17].
- The aircraft has since conducted a live AIM-120 firing with the Experimental Operations Unit[^16].
- Anduril executives said Fury flew in formation with F-35s during a July 2026 exercise at Creech Air Force Base[^20].
- Anduril has begun fit checks for air-to-ground weapons, though these had not been flight-tested as of September 2026[^16].
- The prototype carries two AMRAAMs on underwing pylons[^16]. Anduril states the production FQ-44 will have four external hardpoints for four AMRAAMs[^16].
- **Competitor:** General Atomics' YFQ-42A is the direct Increment 1 comparator[^16][^17]. Trade press labels it both "Vengeance"[^16] and "Dark Merlin"[^17].
- **Follow-on:** Anduril is pursuing the Air Force's CCA follow-on production contract[^8]. No award or loss of that contract, or an Increment 2 bid, is documented in the sources reviewed.
- One outlet reported the Air Force planned to buy roughly 100–150 Increment 1 aircraft and to open an Increment 2 competition in FY2025[^21].

## 6. Strategy and Growth

*Caveat: targets are management's own statements as dated in the text; no dated numeric target could be tied to a source, so there is no targets-versus-consensus table.*

### Stated strategy: product-first, fixed-price, software-defined

Anduril builds complete systems before contracts are awarded, a "product-first" model that IPO Club attributes to an Anduril strategy briefing dated April 2024.[^44] TSG Invest (2026-03-26) describes the same model as funding development with the company's own capital so that it can offer fixed-price contracts rather than cost-plus.[^45] The Lattice operating system is the software layer intended to integrate Ghost, Anvil, Dive-LD and other platforms.[^45][^44] The U.S. Army awarded an enterprise contract on 2026-03-13 that consolidates software procurement.[^3] Inside Unmanned Systems reported on 2026-03-17 that the award positions Lattice as a core platform for counter-UAS operations.[^15] Acquisitions extend the portfolio: Area-I, Blue Force Technologies, AIRS, and space-surveillance firm ExoAnalytic Solutions in March 2026.[^44] New airframes continue to appear. The YFQ-44A collaborative combat aircraft first flew on 2025-10-31, with an Air Force production decision expected in FY2026.[^18] An FAA filing for a CP-200 test aircraft was reported on 2026-09-30.[^22]

### Hyperscale manufacturing and dated capacity targets

The Department of Defense awarded Anduril $14.3 million under Defense Production Act Title III to expand solid rocket motor (SRM) production at the Geisler Building in McHenry, Mississippi.[^46] That award builds on $75 million of Anduril's own capital.[^46] The release carries no date, but it counts the award among DPA awards made since the start of fiscal 2025.[^46]

TSG Invest relays three capacity figures as of 2026-03-26.[^45]

## 14. Recent Developments

*Caveat: The log covers sources dated 2025-10-24 to 2026-10-24 only (the recency rule); the implication column restates the source's own sentence and is not an assessment, and 8-K rows are dated by filing date.*

### Contracts
On 2026-03-13 the U.S. Army awarded Anduril an enterprise contract consolidating procurement of the company's commercially available technologies[^3]. The contract runs 10 years, with a five-year base period and a five-year optional ordering period[^3]. It has a total estimated value of up to $20 billion, which the Army states is a maximum potential value and not an obligated amount[^3]. It replaces more than 120 separate procurement actions and eliminates pass-through charges on subcontracts[^3]. The implication is that the ceiling shows how much the Army could order, while actual revenue depends on task orders placed against it[^3].

### Tests and approvals
The YFQ-44A Collaborative Combat Aircraft first flew on 2025-10-31, as announced by the U.S. Air Force[^18]. The Air Force released few details of the flight, and it said a production decision was still expected during FY2026[^18]. The sources reviewed do not report that decision. On 2026-09-30 FlightGlobal reported an FAA filing in which Anduril requested flight approval for a previously undisclosed CP-200, a Group 3 fixed-wing, propeller-driven uncrewed aircraft intended as an autonomy test platform[^22]. This is a request, not a granted approval[^22]. No failed tests or certifications appear in the sources reviewed.

### Deliveries
No delivery or fielding announcements appear in the sources reviewed. An Army photo caption shows a 1st Cavalry Division operator preparing an Anduril Ghost X at NTC rotation 26‑02 on 2025-11-13, which indicates the system was used in training[^3][^15].

### Financing
An employee tender offer opened on 2026-10-13 and closes on 2026-11-09[^10]. It prices shares at $94.80, implies a valuation of about $90 billion, and is capped at $1.3 billion[^10]. Anduril confirmed the report to CNBC[^10]. The company was valued at $61 billion in a $5 billion round announced in May 2026[^10]. Derived: the implied valuation step-up is about 48% ($90bn ÷ $61bn − 1). The tender gives employees liquidity but raises no primary capital[^10].

### Perspectives
- Inside Unmanned Systems: the Army contract could produce a standardized counter-drone command architecture linking sensors, interceptors and autonomous platforms across agencies[^15].
- U.S. Army (Brig. Gen. Matt Ross, JIATF 401): the contract is a step toward a common counter-UAS interoperability framework[^3].
- U.S. Air Force, as reported by Aero-News Network: a YFQ-44A production decision was expected in FY2026 despite the government shutdown[^18].
