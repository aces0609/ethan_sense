*Caveat: Deal rows are extracted by pattern from 8-K, 10-K/10-Q note and press text; values are as stated at announcement (cash consideration where the source says so), not adjusted for closing terms, and rationale cells quote the source wording rather than assess the deal.*

### Pattern and cadence
The 10-K attributes Northrop Grumman's scale to organic growth and a series of acquisitions and divestitures[^2]. The deals cluster in two periods[^2]:

- **1994–2002 consolidation wave.** The company acquired Grumman, Westinghouse's defense electronics businesses, Litton, Newport News Shipbuilding and TRW[^2].
- **Portfolio reshaping since 2011.** This period includes the Huntington Ingalls spin-off (2011), the Orbital ATK acquisition (2018) and the sale of the IT and mission support services business to Veritas Capital (2021)[^2].

The 10-K lists no other acquisition after TRW in 2002 besides Orbital ATK. That list is selective ("including the following"), so it does not confirm that no other deals occurred[^2].

### The last decade
- **Orbital ATK (2018).** The 10-K describes Orbital ATK as a developer and producer of satellites and other space systems, launch vehicles and missile products[^2].
- **IT and mission support services (2021).** The company completed the sale of this business to Veritas Capital[^2].

The 10-K states that the company acquires or disposes of businesses from time to time. It also states that internal realignments are designed to leverage existing capabilities and improve delivery efficiency[^2]. At December 31, 2025, the company reported four segments: Aeronautics Systems, Defense Systems, Mission Systems and Space Systems[^2].

### Earlier deals and regulatory terms
- **Holding company reorganization (April 2001).** NNG, Inc. acquired the existing company in a one-for-one share exchange[^53]. NNG, Inc. then took the Northrop Grumman Corporation name, and the former parent became Northrop Grumman Systems Corporation[^53].
- **Litton (2001).** The company issued 13,000,000 common and 3,500,000 preferred shares and paid cash for the remaining Litton shares, completing the purchase by June 2001[^14]. Cash paid for businesses, net of cash acquired, was $2,303 million in the first half of 2001[^54]. That figure is not broken out by target.
- **Newport News Shipbuilding (2001–2002).** A tender offer secured about 80.7% of the shares in November 2001, and the company bought the remaining 19.3% on January 18, 2002[^14].
- **TRW (December 2002).** The company issued 69.4 million shares for TRW[^14]. A Department of Justice consent decree addressed competition in electronic satellite payloads and required no divestitures[^14].
- **TRW Automotive sale (February 2003).** The company sold the automotive business to Blackstone for about $4.7 billion[^14].

Citi asked for more detail on 2027[^15]. Greene said all four segments should grow year over year[^15]. He said Defense Systems growth is approaching 10% in 2026 and that Aeronautics growth rests on the B-21[^15]. He put Mission Systems' average annual growth at 3% to 5%[^15].

Ronald Epstein of Bank of America asked about the B-21 production ramp[^15]. CEO Kathy J. Warden said capacity investment will run over the next couple of years, with higher volume coming later in the decade[^15]. She said sustainment and modernization work is also entering the program[^15].
