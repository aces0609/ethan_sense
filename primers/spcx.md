## 3. Products, Services and Technology

*Caveat: product and program descriptions are the company's own IPO prospectus language; status reflects the words used in the filing, not an independent programme assessment, and trade-press pages are context only.*

### Launch and space vehicles

The prospectus places Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy, Starship and Dragon Cargo in its Connectivity segment listing.[^1] It describes Falcon 9 as "production / development," with 620 orbital space launches as its key metric.[^1] The prospectus also attributes an approximately 85% reduction in cost per ton to orbit, versus the historical average, to partial reusability; this is the company's own claim.[^1] Falcon Heavy is described in the prospectus without a status label or metric.[^1] Dragon Cargo carries the status "upgrade."[^1] Starship carries the status "development."[^1]

On the Q3 call, management reported space revenue of $1.8 billion, up 84% sequentially and 6% year over year, and cited a Falcon and Dragon customer manifest.[^10] The prospectus names Boeing, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, Rocket Lab, Firefly Aerospace, Blue Origin and Relativity Space as competitors, but does not assign them to individual vehicles.[^1] SpaceNews reports that SpaceX and Blue Origin are each developing Human Landing System landers for NASA's Artemis program, both to launch under commercial licenses.[^11] That makes Blue Origin the directly comparable rival for Starship's lunar-lander role.[^11]

### Starship regulatory and production milestones

On February 28, 2025, the FAA cleared Starship Flight 8 while the Flight 7 mishap investigation remained open.[^12] SpaceNews reported in August 2025 that SpaceX had FAA approval and a completed failure investigation ahead of Flight 10 on August 24, 2025.[^13] Within the trailing year, the Super Heavy booster for Flight 13 performed a static fire on July 10, 2026, and SpaceNews updated its report on July 13 with an FAA statement.[^14] An October 17, 2026 video discussed a possible double catch of both stages at Starbase on Flight 15.[^15] The FAA required all existing launch licensees to move to its Part 450 rules by March 2026.[^11]

On production, management said Q3 spending accelerated Raptor engine and vehicle production, advanced Gigabay construction and progressed multiple launch pads at Starbase and the Cape.[^10] Management also said it had secured a Louisiana location for the next phase of launch operations.[^10]

### Starlink and government satellites

The prospectus labels Starlink Consumer Broadband "production."[^1] It cites approximately 9,000 broadband satellites as the key metric.[^1] It describes the V2 Mini and V3 satellites without a status label.[^1]

On the Q3 call, management reported:
- 13.6 million Starlink consumer subscribers, with 1.6 million added in Q3.[^10]
- Consumer monthly ARPU of $69, up 5% sequentially.[^10]
- Connectivity revenue of $5.2 billion, up 69% year over year.[^10]
- A start to ramping production of V5 terminal kits during the quarter, which management said eased inventory constraints.[^10]

Management said enterprise and government revenue grew 91% year over year.[^10] It attributed that growth to the Starshield program with the U.S. government and to aviation installations with large carriers.[^10] The prospectus's named connectivity competitors are Comcast and Charter Communications.[^1] Its competitor list names no satellite-broadband operator against Starlink's enterprise, maritime or aviation offerings.[^1]

An undated SpaceX update states that Starship will begin delivering V3 Starlink satellites "this year."[^16] The update claims each such launch will add more than 20 times the capacity of a Falcon V2 launch.[^16] It also says Starship will launch next-generation direct-to-mobile satellites.[^16] These are company targets, not reported results.[^16]

### AI segment

The prospectus lists Grok and the X Platform as "development."[^1] It describes Terrestrial AI Compute, Grok Consumer Products, Grok Chat and Grok Imagine without status labels or metrics.[^1]

- **Capex intensity and free cash flow.** Quarterly free-cash-flow disclosure would weigh Argus's payback view against the cash-burn concerns of Phillip Securities and MoffettNathanson.[^70][^71] That disclosure would need to cover AI build-outs such as the reported $16.8 billion Terafab plan.[^71]
- **AI valuation share.** Segment reporting for the xAI business would bear on CFRA's claim that the AI case carries the valuation.[^72][^71]
