### Business and customers

L3Harris Technologies, Inc. is a Delaware-incorporated defense technology company headquartered in Melbourne, Florida, with about 45,000 employees[^1]. The 10-K states that its capabilities have defense and civil government applications as well as commercial applications[^1]. The 2026 proxy describes the company as delivering technology solutions across the space, air, land, sea and cyber domains for national security customers[^11].

In its Communication Systems segment, the 10-K names the U.S. Department of War and international, federal and state agency customers[^1]. No individual foreign country accounts for more than 5% of total revenue[^1]. The U.S. Government's share of revenue is not quantified in the 10-K passages reviewed.

The predecessor, Harris Corporation, traded on the NYSE as "HRS"[^12]. It has been headquartered in Florida since 1978[^13].

### Fiscal year convention

The fiscal year ends on the Friday nearest December 31[^1]. Fiscal 2025 ended January 2, 2026, and had 52 weeks[^1]. Fiscal 2024 ended January 3, 2025, and had 53 weeks[^1]. Fiscal 2023 ended December 29, 2023, and had 52 weeks[^1]. The extra week in fiscal 2024 affects year-over-year comparisons in the table below.

## 3. Products, Services and Technology

*Caveat: product and program descriptions are the company's own 10-K language; status reflects the words used in the filing, not an independent programme assessment, and trade-press pages are context only.*

### Segment map and space sensors
L3Harris reports through Space & Mission Systems, Communications & Spectrum Dominance and Missile Solutions, and in April 2026 it appointed a single leader over the first two segments[^14][^3]. Fiscal 2025 contractual backlog was $38.7 billion, and the company expects to recognize about 45% of it by the end of fiscal 2026[^1]. In Q1 2026, Space & Mission Systems revenue was $3 billion, up 24%, helped by a milestone on a classified program[^3].

- **Tracking Layer Tranche 0 (delivered):** L3Harris delivered four prototype missile-tracking satellites under a $193 million contract[^15].
- **Tracking Layer Tranche 1 (in production):** The company holds a $700 million contract for 16 satellites plus ground systems, using Maxar buses[^15]. As of a January 2026 photo, the satellites were in production at Palm Bay, Florida[^16]. A 2025 launch had been scheduled, and no launch confirmation appears in the sources reviewed[^15].
- **Tracking Layer Tranche 2 (on contract):** Each vendor supplies 16 wide-field-of-view satellites and two fire-control satellites, with launch projected for 2027[^17].
- **Next missile warning/defense tranche (awarded July 13, 2026):** SDA awarded about $1.75 billion, shared between L3Harris and Sierra Space, for 36 satellites[^16]. The sources reviewed do not give L3Harris's individual share.

### Communications, EW and ISR aircraft
- **Communications & Spectrum Dominance:** Q1 2026 revenue was $1.9 billion, up 3%[^3]. The company booked $460 million of international resilient communications orders with three NATO countries in the quarter[^3].
- **Viper Shield F-16 EW suite (LRIP):** The suite cleared production readiness review and was approved for low-rate production in November 2025[^18]. At that point the company planned full-rate production by Q1 2026 and first kit deliveries in December 2026[^18]. The order book is 219 systems for seven foreign military sales customers: 168 for new-build Block 70/72 jets and 51 retrofits[^18]. The sources reviewed do not confirm that the full-rate transition has occurred.
- **Missionized business jets (awarded):** L3Harris won an international program worth more than $2.2 billion, with an initial order of $726 million[^3]. It also won two Royal Canadian Air Force contracts totaling about $700 million[^3].

### Missile Solutions (Aerojet Rocketdyne) propulsion
Aerojet Rocketdyne now operates as Missile Solutions[^2]. Q1 2026 segment revenue was $1 billion, up 18%, at a 12.5% operating margin[^3].

The Department of War made a $1 billion direct investment in Missile Solutions through its Industrial Base Analysis and Sustainment authority[^2]. The stated purpose is to fund solid rocket motor capacity expansion for PAC-3, THAAD, Tomahawk and Standard Missile[^2]. The terms provide for creating a separately traded missile company, and L3Harris has confidentially filed an S-1 for an IPO of the unit under the name "Axyv"[^2][^3].

On September 8, 2026, L3Harris announced a PAC-3 MSE propulsion contract[^19]. As of Q1 2026, management said $25 billion of Munitions Acceleration Council orders were still under negotiation[^3].

### Next-generation platforms and competitors
- **F/A-XX:** The Navy awarded the program to Boeing, with full-scale development valued at more than $20 billion; Northrop Grumman was the losing bidder[^20][^21].
- **F-47:** Boeing won the Air Force's F-47 fighter in March 2025[^21].
- **L3Harris role:** None of these sources identifies an L3Harris role, prime or sub, on either program.

The 10-K names its principal competitors as BAE Systems, Boeing, General Dynamics, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, RTX and Thales, plus newer entrants Anduril, Ursa Major and Silvus Technologies[^1].
> Conflict: A competitor list compiled from the same 10-K also includes Palantir, Shield Capital and Amazon Kuiper, which do not appear in the 10-K passage reviewed[^1].

The clearest head-to-head comparison is in missile tracking:
- **Tranche 1:** Northrop Grumman and RTX build satellites under separate contracts alongside L3Harris[^15].
- **Tranche 2:** Lockheed Martin pairs Terran Orbital buses with General Atomics infrared payloads[^17]. Sierra Space uses 18 Geost payloads[^17]. L3Harris's design uses Maxar buses, per the Tranche 1 contract[^15].

On EW, Viper Shield equips F-16s that Lockheed Martin builds for export[^18].

## 7. Key Acquisition History

*Caveat: Deal rows are extracted by pattern from 8-K, 10-K/10-Q note and press text; values are as stated at announcement (cash consideration where the source says so), not adjusted for closing terms, and rationale cells quote the source wording rather than assess the deal.*

### 2019 L3–Harris merger and the required Night Vision sale

The merger was all-stock, and L3 shares converted into 1.3 L3Harris shares each after trading closed on June 28, 2019.[^45] Harris reported about $6 billion of annual revenue and L3 reported 2018 sales of $10.2 billion.[^45] On a derived basis ($6.0B + $10.2B), the combined pro forma revenue was about $16.2 billion. Antitrust clearance required Harris to divest its Night Vision business to Elbit Systems Ltd. under a previously announced agreement, with the sale expected to complete after the merger.[^45][^46] Night vision remains in the portfolio through the Integrated Vision Solutions sector of Communication Systems, which makes helmet-mounted goggles with image intensifier tubes.[^1]

### Aerojet Rocketdyne

L3Harris signed the merger agreement on December 17, 2022, at $58.00 in cash per share.[^47] The agreement added $0.0025 per share for each day after September 17, 2023 that closing slipped.[^47] The deal closed on July 28, 2023, so the ticking fee did not apply and holders received $58.00 per share.[^48] A $2.4 billion 364-day revolving facility, dated March 10, 2023, was set up partly to fund the purchase price.[^49]

### Pattern and recent activity

The 10-K describes acquisitions, divestitures and realignments across segments as a recurring tool.[^1] It reports that the segment count fell from four to three starting in fiscal 2026.[^1] The fiscal 2026 10-Qs describe a strategic investment by the Department of War in an L3Harris subsidiary, made through preferred stock, embedded derivatives and warrants.[^50] That investment created a $386 million program investment intangible asset in the Missile Solutions segment.[^50] The asset will be amortized over 20 years once a milestone is reached.[^50] The company's revolving credit agreements bar using loan proceeds for hostile acquisitions.[^51][^49] The current $2.5 billion five-year revolver was established on February 18, 2025.[^52]

### Perspectives

- U.S. Department of Justice: without the divestiture, the merger would have removed competition between the only two suppliers of U.S. military-grade image intensifier tubes.[^46]
- U.S. Department of Justice: the original structure would have given the combined company a monopoly over a component critical to military night vision.[^46]

### Bull themes and management answers

**Missiles and solid rocket motors.** On the Q4-2025 call, Morgan Stanley's Kristine Liwag asked about further long-term agreements like PAC-3 and THAAD[^67]. She also asked whether Missile Solutions could grow three to five times larger[^67]. Kubasik answered that L3Harris is the only provider of propulsion and divert and attitude control systems for THAAD, and that such agreements were starting to take form[^67]. On the Q1-2026 call, Wolfe Research asked about the timing of framework negotiations[^3]. Kubasik described the framework agreement as akin to a term sheet, negotiated as solid rocket motor supplier to Lockheed and Raytheon[^3]. Since then, L3Harris has announced a PAC-3 MSE propulsion contract (2026-09-08) and the opening of a new solid rocket motor facility[^19][^68].

**Space, Golden Dome and backlog.** Bank of America asked about the space business[^3]. Kubasik said missile warning and tracking and the classified work are "growing very, very well," with many Golden Dome opportunities[^3]. On the Q4-2025 call, Baird asked about the total Golden Dome opportunity[^67]. Kubasik cited $25 billion for Golden Dome within $155 billion of reconciliation funding[^67]. He added that space-based interceptors would likely be the slowest piece to develop[^67]. On the Q1-2026 call, Goldman Sachs asked about pull-forward[^3]. Kubasik pointed to $60–70 billion of backlog within twelve months once the MAC programs are in[^3].

**Communications.** Bernstein asked about communications[^3]. Kubasik said margins rose while the company invested, and that a new radio would roll out shortly[^3]. JPMorgan asked about budget participation[^3]. Kubasik cited a $515 million 2027 budget for the Army HMS program, with similar amounts outlined for the following five years[^3].

### Bear themes and management answers

**Free cash flow versus capital intensity.** On the Q4-2025 call, Wolfe Research asked whether the step-up in missile investment was large enough[^67]. Bedingfield said 2026 CapEx would rise to about 2.5% of sales, roughly $600 million, which is 35–40% above 2025[^67]. He held 2026 free cash flow guidance at $3 billion and declined to give a 2027 CapEx figure[^67].

**Portfolio transactions and guidance comparability.** The Q1-2026 guidance excludes three transactions: the planned Missile Solutions IPO, the Department of War investment, and the planned sale of a majority stake in the space propulsion business[^3]. Management said it would update guidance when those transactions occur[^3]. In answer to Citigroup on the Q4-2025 call, Kubasik said L3Harris would continue to consolidate, own and control Missile Solutions after the IPO[^67]. He also guided the remaining company to solid mid-single-digit growth[^67].

**Budget dependence.** TD Cowen asked whether new priorities would be funded in the FY27 request[^67]. Kubasik said the company had no insight into the FY27 budget[^67]. The 10-K warns that losing one or more large government contracts could significantly harm the business[^1].

**Reported results.** MarketBeat cites "third quarter" EPS of $3.13 against a $2.80 consensus, and FY2026 EPS guidance of $11.80–12.00[^7]. Yahoo Finance, however, lists the next earnings date as Oct 29, 2026[^33]. Because of that, the quarter MarketBeat refers to cannot be confirmed from the sources reviewed[^7][^33].

## 14. Recent Developments

*Caveat: The log covers sources dated 2025-10-22 to 2026-10-22 only (the recency rule); the implication column restates the source's own sentence and is not an assessment, and 8-K rows are dated by filing date.*

> Conflict: chief executive officer reported as Kimberly Fields[^42] (section 8) and Christopher Kubasik[^89] (section 14); sources differ and are not reconciled.

### Deals and portfolio
- **13 January.** L3Harris announced terms for a proposed $1 billion Department of War convertible preferred investment in its Missile Solutions business, with an IPO of that business intended in the second half of 2026[^90]. *Implication: the missile unit is being set up as a separately valued, government-backed entity.*
- **16 April.** Definitive agreements were signed through Aerojet Rocketdyne Holdings[^14]. The preferred converts at 80% of the IPO price, and warrants convert into rights to 3% of the company at 110% of the IPO price[^14]. The investor is expected to own less than 10% after the IPO[^14]. *Implication: the discount and warrants set a dilution cost on the listing.*
- **8 September.** Lockheed Martin awarded L3Harris an undefinitized, multi-year contract valued at $4.7 billion over seven years for PAC-3 MSE propulsion[^19]. The company called it its largest PAC-3 propulsion award[^19]. Two new Camden, Arkansas facilities are expected to be operational in 2027[^19]. *Implication: the value is not yet definitized, and execution depends on capacity that is not yet built.*

### Results
- Earnings releases came out for Q4 2025 (29 January), Q1 (30 April) and Q2 2026 (29 July)[^91][^62][^41].
- MiGFlug reports 2025 revenue of $21.9 billion[^92].

### Governance and leadership
- **4 February.** Chairman and CEO Christopher Kubasik adopted a Rule 10b5-1 trading plan[^89].
- **16 March.** Kenneth Sharp became CFO, succeeding Kenneth Bedingfield, who now leads Missile Solutions[^93].
- **Same date.** Sam Mehta took charge of both the SMS and CSD segments after CSD head Jonathan Rambeau announced his departure[^53].
- **11 May annual meeting.** Shareholders approved named-executive pay by 148,177,289 votes for to 7,936,376 against[^54]. Derived support is about 94.7%, calculated as for ÷ (for + against + abstain)[^54]. Shareholders rejected a proposal on calling special meetings by 51,888,937 to 104,022,702[^54].
- **17 August.** Mehta was named President and CEO and joined the Board, with a $1,250,000 base salary and a $13,250,000 long-term incentive target[^6]. The SMS and CSD segments he ran account for about 80% of revenue[^6]. *Implication: this was a second leadership change in five months, and its effect on the IPO timetable is undisclosed.*
- **21 October.** The Board grew from eleven to twelve seats with the appointment of Kimberly Fields, CEO of ATI[^42].

### Perspectives
- MiGFlug describes L3Harris as "taking the business apart again" less than three years after the $4.7 billion Aerojet Rocketdyne purchase. It cites a January 2026 agreement to sell a majority of space propulsion to AE Industrial Partners[^92].
